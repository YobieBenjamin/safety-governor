# Audit of the Safety Governor design, October 2026

A deep pass over every claim in `docs/`, `govsim/`, `rtl/`, `hw/` and the blog's Part 4,
looking for three things: statements that were not true (hallucinations), conditions the
model made true by construction rather than by mechanism (fake conditions), and claims
nobody can verify yet that were written as if they were facts. Each finding says what was
wrong, what was checked against which source, and what changed. The last section lists what
is still unverified and should be treated as a design target, a class estimate, or an ask.

## 1. Hallucinations found and corrected

| # | What the design said | What is true, and the source | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Production signatures on ECDSA P-256 "because it matches GPU attestation" | NVIDIA's GPU attestation chain is ECDSA P-384 over SHA-384: the local verifier in NVIDIA's open nvtrust SDK checks 96-byte signatures with SHA-384. P-384 is also the curve of AMD's SEV-SNP VCEK | `04-specification.md` §1 and §7: P-384 is the production curve; P-256 only for TPM-class endpoints; the model's Ed25519 is labelled as the model's choice |
| 2 | Device-provenance signature "tens of microseconds" / "60 µs"; the bench priced it that way | No source. An ECDSA P-384 signature on an OTBN-class coprocessor is in the low milliseconds; nobody has measured the governor's engine | `04` §9: a budget of under 2 ms per signature, records batched under one Merkle root per 10 ms window; `govsim/bench.py` charges 1 ms; the blog's "eighty milliseconds, three hundredths of a percent" became "about one second, four tenths of a percent", with the short-response case stated |
| 3 | The silicon flow was "OpenLane 2", run from the `ghcr.io/efabless/openlane2` Docker image | Efabless closed in March 2025. The flow continued as LibreLane under the FOSSi Foundation (same authors, same configuration format), installed with `pip install librelane` and run with `librelane --dockerized`, with the `ciel` PDK manager | `hw/openlane` renamed `hw/librelane`; `Makefile`, `hw/README.md`, `08-silicon-plan.md`, `README.md` and the blog updated; two keys the new flow rejects removed from `config.json` |
| 4 | Shuttles: "the successor to chipIgnite" | ChipFoundry, started by Efabless's founders, runs sky130 MPW slots (first tapeout September 2025; a shuttle runs when at least half its slots are sold). Tiny Tapeout continued independently on IHP SG13G2 and, through ChipFoundry, sky130 | `08` §2.4 rewritten with those facts and cost classes |
| 5 | Lithography text named a specific exposure wavelength and mask count for sky130 | Not public in that form. What is public: 130 nm is a mature node printed with deep-ultraviolet steppers, no immersion, no multi-patterning | `08` §2.3 softened to what is public |
| 6 | NVL72 compute trays carry "two or four GPUs" | Four GPUs per compute tray | `01-architecture.md` |
| 7 | DICE identity: the device key was derived from the UDS alone, independent of firmware | DICE derives the DeviceID from the UDS and the first measured layer (the loader); the alias key from the next layer (the firmware) | `govsim/root.py` boot: `cdi0 = KDF(UDS, MEAS0)`, DeviceID from `cdi0`, `cdi1 = KDF(cdi0, MEAS1)`, alias key from `cdi1`; new tests |
| 8 | "Every GPU slot already has a power-brake signal", "PWRBRK# by specification on data-center GPUs" | NVIDIA GPUs have a hardware power-brake input (nvidia-smi reports "HW Power Brake Slowdown"). On PCIe cards it is the optional PWRBRK# edge-connector signal, which many boards do not wire. On SXM baseboards it is a board signal where one is provided. NVIDIA's product briefs document the SMBPBI power cap as settable out of band and persistent across driver loads and system boots | `03-interfaces.md` §2, `hw/pcb/rack-integration.md` §2, the blog: hardware brake where wired, SMBPBI power cap as the software-honoured fallback, reset as the hard fallback; per-slot PWR_EN only where the board has it, recorded in the policy |
| 9 | "All PCIe switch families expose per-port enable over a management sideband" | Family-specific: Microchip Switchtec exposes management in-band and over UART, two-wire/SMBus and EJTAG, with per-port hot-plug controllers and downstream port containment; Broadcom PEX has a management port and I2C/SPI. ACS is a standard switch feature | `03` §3 states the per-family facts and that the exact set is confirmed on the chosen board |
| 10 | NVSwitch out-of-band bus gives the governor "reset, power and telemetry" and port control | What exists: SMBPBI-based out-of-band monitoring of NVSwitch, the baseboard's HMC and reset lines, and Fabric Manager (in-band) for routing and NVLink partitions (partition keys, activate/deactivate APIs in shared-NVSwitch and vGPU modes). An out-of-band port disable is not documented | `03` §3: partition membership via Fabric Manager and reset today; OOB port disable moved to the asks (§9) |
| 11 | DC-SCM described as "the standard card that carries the baseboard controller" on every system board | OCP's DC-SCM is a module standard for the BMC and the platform root of trust, on the DC-SCI connector (PCIe, eSPI, SPI, I2C/I3C, USB, and the LTPI LVDS tunnel for sideband signals). Many, not all, current boards have it | `03` §1 and the blog: "many modern system boards", with the connector and tunnel named |
| 12 | "Ed25519 on OTBN" | OpenTitan's OTBN programs are ECDSA P-256 and P-384, X25519, RSA, SHA-2/3, ML-KEM and ML-DSA; there is no Ed25519 program | `08` §1 table; `README.md` status |
| 13 | The demo printed "propagated in 100 µs" as if measured | It is the model's `sense_period` parameter | `govsim/cli.py` prints it as a model parameter; `02` §1 says the bench measures it |
| 14 | Register and prose named the power-cut lever as "12V_EN" in places and "PWR_EN" in others | One name | `04` §2, `05`, `07` use power brake, PERST#, PWR_EN |
| 15 | Bring-up plan in `08` §2.5 numbered (6) then (8) | Typo | Renumbered |

## 2. Fake conditions found and closed

| # | What the model did | Why it was fake | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An epoch straggler was expelled only because the test partitioned it off the fabric; a reachable governor that simply failed to acknowledge would have kept running on the old rules | R9 says a member that cannot acknowledge within the bound is halted, not left on the old rules; the mechanism was never exercised | `Rack.change_epoch(image, silent=...)` proposes to every reachable governor and commits to those that acknowledged; heartbeats carry the new epoch; `Governor._heartbeat` refuses a heartbeat whose epoch it has not accepted (`POLICY_STALE_EPOCH`), so the straggler's timer expires and it enters safe mode. `tests/test_h14_partition.py::test_reachable_straggler_is_expelled_by_the_epoch_bound_heartbeat` |
| 2 | Roster rejection was tested only with a fake vendor certificate; a genuine chip running unapproved firmware was never tried | The DICE claim is that firmware changes the key; the test had to show a real chip with the wrong firmware failing to join | `Rack.rogue_governor_with_bad_firmware()`; `tests/test_h12_fabric_auth.py::test_genuine_chip_with_unapproved_firmware_cannot_join`; `tests/test_h15_tamper.py` checks that modified firmware changes the alias key and a modified loader changes the device identity |
| 3 | The overhead test passed because the signature was priced at a figure nobody had measured | A model that prices its most expensive operation optimistically proves nothing | Priced at the budget; the H9 test now also asserts that short responses exceed the one-percent bound unless batched, and that batching brings them under it (`concurrent_sequences` in `bench.py`) |
| 4 | The functional model's durable store deep-copied the entire state on every save, so the simulation was quadratic in log size and the bench timed out on realistic runs | Not a correctness fault, but it hid the bench's behaviour at scale | `govsim/store.py` snapshots with `pickle` (35× faster, same save-before-return and power-loss semantics) |

## 4. Still unverified: targets, estimates and asks, not facts

- **Whether a given SXM baseboard brings out a hardware power-brake signal, and whether it has per-slot power control.** Board-specific; the design records both per board and the floor is reset.
- **An out-of-band port disable on NVSwitch.** An ask. Today's authority is partition membership through Fabric Manager plus switch reset.
- **Halt-loop timing** (100 µs sense period, 1 ms end to end over 72 governors). Design targets until the Phase 2 bench measures them.
- **Signature latency on the governor's own crypto engine** (budget 2 ms). Unmeasured; the batching exists so the design does not depend on the figure.
- **The one-percent overhead on a whole protected deployment** (R32). A model today; the Phase 2 measurement replaces it.
- **Cost and schedule** (low five figures per shuttle slot; about a year to a measured prototype; low millions and 18 to 24 months for product silicon). Class estimates, not quotes.
- **Side-channel resistance of the crypto engines** in the prototype. Not proven; the product node enables countermeasures.
- **The prototype's tamper resistance.** None by design: sky130 has no open fuse macro, so the UDS is injected at power-up by an external secure element. Recorded in the assumptions register.
