| What | Measured |

| Governor handles KEY_RENEW | 0.62 ms |

## What this does and does not show

- The key handling is cheap: milliseconds per release, and renewals cost the governor well under a second per hour.
- The load-time cost is the decryption, and it scales with the weights. The software number here is one core of this machine's CPU standing in for a GPU decrypting in its own hardware in confidential-computing mode. Spread over cores, or done by the GPU's engines as weights stream in, it overlaps with reading them from storage; how much is the measurement a real GPU has to make, and it goes on the page when it is made.
- The simulated GPU enforces its own lease and wipes its key. A real GPU would do that in firmware NVIDIA writes, so "the GPU stops by itself when the lease runs out" is an ask of NVIDIA, not something this design can force.
- It protects weights that stay sealed. Open weights, or weights already copied in the clear, are outside its reach.
