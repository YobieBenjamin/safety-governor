Building a road is itself an action that needs a ticket. This audits a rulebook: every action is labelled as acting within an already-signed boundary or opening a new one, and an open action that is auto-approved is a road the agent can take on its own. It is a reachability check over the rules, not an agent rewarded to find a way through.

## Finding A1-a (reference rulebook)

`model.export.70b` is auto and names the exact evaluated model but **not a destination**, so the evaluated model can be auto-exported anywhere. Its sibling `weights.write.own` is destination-locked to the model's own storage; the export rule is not. The audit flags it, and the fix is the same lock: give `model.export.70b` a `destination` constraint. (Mitigation today: the bytes still cross a governed egress that the rule does not grant, so this is a belt-and-suspenders gap, not an open door. It is on the record either way.)

## What this shows, and what it cannot

- Every road but the one found is held for humans or refused; the auto actions act only within boundaries someone already signed, and none grants a new capability, so chaining them opens nothing new.
- The one road it did find is the point of the exercise: the audit is a CI check meant to run against whatever rulebook ships and fail on exactly this kind of gap. The fix is shown to close it.
- What it cannot cover, as `docs/23` concedes: persuading a person (a held road still ends at a human who can say yes), and a model on ungoverned hardware. The audit bounds the roads inside the gate, not outside.
