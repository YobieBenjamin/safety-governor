# P3b · The containment rate: how little can leave a governed rack

Generated by `python -m govsim.containment --write`. The defensive side of the replication question (a reader's comment on Part 6); companion to the covert-channel budget (`tests/test_h10_leakage.py`, issue P3).

**Result: every check holds** (6 of 6).

The gate never inspects content. It decides by destination and volume at each exit. So the question is not whether the chip can tell an AI's packets from any other packets (it does not try) but how many bytes an hour can leave through the permitted channels, and whether that moves a model that matters.

## The ceiling, measured

| Rulebook | Signed channel | Unpresented slack | Off-roster flow | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reference rulebook | 200,000 B/h | 1,048,576 B | refused | 1,248,576 B/h |
| sandbox (tightest) | 6,135 B/h | 4,096 B | refused | 10,231 B/h |
| busy service (loose) | 26,200 B/h | 16,777,216 B | refused | 16,803,416 B/h |

## What that ceiling means for moving weights

At the tightest (sandbox) ceiling, time to move a copy of the weights out through the permitted channels:

| Weights | Size | Time at the sandbox ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 70B model, fp16 | 140 GB | 1,562 years |
| 70B model, 4-bit | 35 GB | 391 years |
| 8B model, 4-bit | 4 GB | 50 years |

And under a busy-service rulebook with a generous budget, the honest other direction:

| Weights | Time at the busy-service ceiling |
|---|---|
| 70B model, fp16 | 347 days |
| 70B model, 4-bit | 87 days |
| 8B model, 4-bit | 11 days |

## What this shows, and what it does not

- The gate needs no idea what a packet is. Bytes to anything off the signed roster are refused at the exit, and the bytes that may leave are capped. Under a tight policy the cap makes moving even a small model take longer than a lifetime.
- Sealed weights (W1) make this a double lock: a copy that did leave would be ciphertext without the key, which the governor releases only to attested hardware. The plaintext case is the one the ceiling guards.
- The budget is the defence, so a policy loose enough to run a busy service is loose enough to leak a small model over time. That is the real tension, and the number is on the page rather than hidden: a deployer picks the budget knowing what it costs.
- What the chip does not touch, as `docs/23` already concedes: a small agent that carries no weights, just code that calls some other model elsewhere, is tiny and fits any useful channel; and a model already running on ungoverned hardware is outside every exit this measures. The gate contains what leaves a governed rack. It is not a boundary around the open Internet, and the design says so.
