# EXPERIMENT-CONTAINMENT · The containment rate: how little can leave a governed rack

Generated by `python -m govsim.containment --write`. The defensive side of the replication question (a reader's comment on Part 6); companion to the covert-channel budget (`tests/test_h10_leakage.py`, issue WAVE-COVERT-CHANNEL-BUDGET).

**Result: every check holds** (9 of 9).

The gate never inspects content. It decides by destination and volume at each exit. So the question is not whether the chip can tell an AI's packets from any other packets (it does not try) but how many bytes an hour can leave through the permitted channels, and whether that moves a model that matters.

The numbers below are measured at the enforced boundary: bytes that actually LEFT through the egress path (`egress.bytes_delivered`), under the policy the governor is actually running (its live limits are read back and asserted before measuring). They are arithmetic on the simulator's budgets, a design target, not a measurement of silicon that does not exist yet.

## The ceiling, measured at the boundary

The sustained rate is the per-hour signed-response delivery. The burst is the one-time unpresented slack (a cumulative host-minus-presented gap, bounded once, with no hourly refill). They are reported separately and never summed: a one-time budget is not a rate.

| Rulebook | Installed (bytes/queries) | Sustained (delivered) | One-time burst | Off-roster flow | Finite ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reference rulebook | 200,000 B / 50 q | 200,000 B/h | 1,048,576 B | refused | holds |
| sandbox (tightest) | 4,096 B / 10 q | 4,090 B/h | 4,096 B | refused | holds |
| busy service (loose) | 52,428,800 B / 100,000 q | 52,428,800 B/h | 16,777,216 B | refused | holds |

## Every permitted channel, inventoried

The signed-response channel is not the only way out. A listed-recipient `message.send` is a permitted channel too; with an unbounded body it has no finite byte ceiling, so the finite-ceiling claim fails until the body is bounded and counted. The busy and reference rulebooks here bound it (4 KB bodies, 60 per minute); the sandbox gates messaging to humans.

| Rulebook | Channel | Bounded | Sustained |
|---|---|---|---|
| reference rulebook | signed response (egress) | yes | 200,000 B/h |
| reference rulebook | message.send (listed recipient) | yes | 14,745,600 B/h |
| sandbox (tightest) | signed response (egress) | yes | 4,090 B/h |
| busy service (loose) | signed response (egress) | yes | 52,428,800 B/h |
| busy service (loose) | message.send (listed recipient) | yes | 14,745,600 B/h |

## What that sustained ceiling means for moving weights

At the tightest (sandbox) sustained ceiling, time to move a copy of the weights out through the permitted channels (the one-time burst is negligible against a model this size):

|---|---|---|

## Corrections on the record (external audit F12, F13)

- The earlier table recorded 6,135 and 26,200 B/h for the sandbox and busy cases. Those were the reference and loop values: the named policy was never installed, because the code changed `policy_kw` after construction and `bring_up()` does not reload the policy. The numbers above install the named policy and read its limits back first.
- The earlier "50 years / centuries / 11 days" interpretations were computed from a ceiling that summed a one-time burst onto a per-hour rate, and from signing calls rather than delivered bytes. Those interpretations are withdrawn; the corrected sustained figures are above.
- The old measurement counted successful `sign_output` calls. A provenance signature with no release moves no bytes; the figures above are read back from the egress boundary after the complete release path.

## What this shows, and what it does not

- The gate needs no idea what a packet is. Bytes to anything off the signed roster are refused at the exit, and the bytes that may leave are capped. Under a tight policy the cap makes moving even a small model take longer than a lifetime.
- Sealed weights (EXPERIMENT-ORIGIN-GATE) make this a double lock: a copy that did leave would be ciphertext without the key, which the governor releases only to attested hardware. The plaintext case is the one the ceiling guards.
- The ceiling is only finite if EVERY permitted channel is bounded. A deployer who leaves a listed-recipient message body unbounded has no byte ceiling at all, whatever the signed-response budget says.
- The budget is the defence, so a policy loose enough to run a busy service is loose enough to leak a small model over time. That is the real tension, and the number is on the page rather than hidden: a deployer picks the budget knowing what it costs.
- What the chip does not touch, as `docs/23` already concedes: a small agent that carries no weights, just code that calls some other model elsewhere, is tiny and fits any useful channel; and a model already running on ungoverned hardware is outside every exit this measures. The gate contains what leaves a governed rack. It is not a boundary around the open Internet, and the design says so.
